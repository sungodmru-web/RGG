2.3.5 Technological and Geopolitical Drivers 
Technological developments further reinforce the transition. Improvements in 
cultivation techniques, product standardisation, and traceability systems are making 
regulated markets more viable. Digital tools and data systems enhance the ability of 
governments to monitor and manage complex supply chains. These developments 
support a shift towards governance models that rely on oversight rather than 
prohibition alone. Seed-to-sale tracking systems, laboratory testing protocols, and digital 
licensing platforms provide the informational infrastructure necessary for effective 
regulation. While these systems require investment, they enable a level of oversight that 
was previously unattainable. For LMIC, proportionate and scalable technologies offer 
pathways to regulatory capability without requiring the same level of investment as high-
income jurisdictions. 
Geopolitical and trade dynamics are also beginning to shape cannabis policy. As global 
markets evolve, competition for market access, standards compliance, and investment 
flows is increasing. For some countries, cannabis is emerging as part of a broader 
strategy to position themselves within global value chains, linking trade policy with 
economic diplomacy. However, these drivers do not operate in isolation. Their 
interaction creates cumulative pressure for change. Scientific evidence informs public 
debate, which in turn shapes political priorities. Economic opportunities influence 
policy choices, while institutional constraints limit the range of feasible options. The 
shift from prohibition to governance is best interpreted as the outcome of multiple, 
mutually reinforcing dynamics. 
This convergence of drivers does not lead to a single outcome. Instead, it produces a 
diversity of policy pathways, reflecting differences in context, priorities, and institutional 
capacity. Some countries move towards comprehensive regulatory systems, while others 
adopt more incremental approaches. Understanding this diversity requires moving 
beyond binary classifications and towards a more flexible analytical framework—the 
subject of the next section. 
2.4 The Governance Continuum 
Debates on cannabis policy are often framed in binary terms, opposing prohibition to 
legalisation. While this distinction has analytical value, it is increasingly insufficient to 
capture the diversity and complexity of emerging policy approaches. In practice, 
countries are not simply choosing between two discrete options; they are positioning 
themselves along a spectrum of regulatory arrangements that combine elements of 
control, tolerance, and market governance.
