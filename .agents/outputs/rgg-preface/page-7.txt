The book comprises 20 chapters organised into five interrelated parts, each addressing 
a key dimension of cannabis governance as follows. 
Part I: Cannabis at the Crossroads (Chapters 1–5) 
It anchors cannabis within its historical, cultural, and political contexts. Chapter 1 
frames reform as a governance transition rather than a binary legal choice. Chapter 2 
traces the evolution from prohibition's rigid architecture to a more nuanced continuum 
of governance. Chapter 3 reveals cannabis as a medicinal, industrial, and cultural asset 
across civilisations — one which embedded knowledge systems were disrupted by 
twentieth-century prohibition. Chapter 4 offers a global ethnography of cannabis as 
healer, sacrament, and cultural companion. Chapter 5 examines the politics of 
knowledge and legitimacy, showing how colonialism and prohibition established 
epistemic hierarchies that continue to marginalise traditional knowledge. 
Legitimacy is foundational. Where formal rules align with cultural norms and lived 
realities, compliance follows; where they diverge, informal systems persist, and public 
trust erodes. No legal reform survives long without it. 
Part II: Science, Health, and Risk Governance (Chapters 6–8) 
It examines cannabis through the integrated lenses of scientific rediscovery, clinical 
application, and public health. Chapter 6 traces the scientific renaissance, from the 
isolation of THC and CBD to the discovery of the Endocannabinoid system, and 
addresses tensions between pharmaceutical and whole-plant models. Chapter 7 
translates these foundations into clinical practice, reviewing evidence for cannabis-based 
therapies and analysing regulatory models for medical access. Chapter 8 addresses public 
health and risk governance, examining prevention, harm reduction, and the interactions 
between cannabis and alcohol, tobacco, and opioids. 
Scientific evidence is necessary but not sufficient. Effective translation into policy 
requires coherent regulation, institutional capacity, and sustained attention to equity of 
access, markedly for populations historically excluded from formal healthcare systems. 
Part III: Sustainable Cannabis Economy (Chapters 9–11) 
It examines the cannabis economy through the lenses of economic governance, 
production systems, and global market dynamics. Chapter 9 establishes the foundations 
for a sustainable cannabis economy, framing it as shaped by formalisation, value 
distribution, and the alignment of economic, social, and environmental objectives. 
Chapter 10 positions industrial hemp as a strategic interface between agriculture, 
industry, and green industrialisation. Chapter 11 analyses global value chains, trade 
flows, and market structures, demonstrating that cultivation captures only a fraction of 
final value while processing, branding, and downstream functions concentrate returns. 
Economic potential is contingent on governance choices. Countries that supply raw 
biomass while lacking the infrastructure to process, brand, or certify risk permanent 
subordination in global value chains — not because of geography or climate, but 
because of governance gaps that are entirely addressable.
