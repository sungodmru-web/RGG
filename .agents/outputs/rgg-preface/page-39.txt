2.5.2 Medical Cannabis Frameworks 
A second category comprises medical cannabis frameworks. These systems allow access 
to cannabis for therapeutic purposes under controlled conditions, typically involving 
prescription requirements, licensed producers, and regulated distribution channels. 
Medical frameworks introduce elements of quality control, standardisation, and 
oversight, thereby improving patient safety. 
Medical cannabis programmes vary significantly in design. Some jurisdictions, such as 
Germany, operate pharmacy-based distribution systems where patients obtain cannabis 
products through prescriptions filled at licensed pharmacies. Others, such as Canada, 
allow licensed producers to sell directly to patients through mail-order systems. The 
scope of qualifying conditions, product types permitted, and physician involvement also 
differ across jurisdictions.  
Yet the scope of medical systems is generally limited, and access can be constrained by 
regulatory complexity, cost, and institutional capacity. In some contexts, medical 
systems have also served as transitional mechanisms, paving the way for broader 
regulatory reforms (WHO, 2019). Medical cannabis frameworks often build regulatory 
expertise and establish testing infrastructure. They also help normalise cannabis as a 
regulated product, laying the foundation for broader access over time. 
2.5.3 State-Controlled Systems 
A third model is state-controlled or monopoly-based systems. In this approach, the state 
retains direct control over key segments of the cannabis value chain, including 
production, distribution, and retail. The objective is to maintain a high degree of 
oversight while reducing the role of private actors. Uruguay's cannabis regulation, 
implemented in 2013, exemplifies this approach. The government regulates production, 
sets prices, and controls distribution through pharmacies. 
Registered users may also cultivate cannabis at home or participate in cannabis social 
clubs. The model prioritises public health and regulatory control over commer-
cialisation, but requires sustained state involvement and administrative capacity. This 
approach can limit market concentration and provide clearer regulatory oversight, 
although it may be less responsive to market dynamics. It also offers greater 
opportunities to monitor product quality, protect consumers, and generate reliable data. 
Its effectiveness ultimately depends on public institutions operating efficiently, 
transparently, and consistently.
