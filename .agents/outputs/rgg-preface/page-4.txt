About the Authors 
 Dr Soobaschand (Sunil) Sweenarain 
A senior natural resource economist with over three 
decades of international experience in sustainable 
development, renewable resource management, economic 
governance, and policy advisory. Born and raised in 
Mauritius, he has centred his career primarily on the 
African continent, where he has led and advised on
complex programmes in resource management, regional 
cooperation, and economic integration.  
Rooted in the global sustainability principles driven by the blue-green economy 
paradigm, Dr Sweenarain has provided expertise in large-scale project design, 
institutional and human capacity building, and strategic policy advisory services. He has 
worked extensively with governments, regional organisations, and international 
partners, contributing to the creation of shared value at national, regional, and global 
levels. He is the author of numerous technical and strategic reports. 
This book reflects Dr Sweenarain engagement with systems-level analysis, governance 
design, and development pathways, brought into dialogue with debates on cannabis 
regulation, bio-economy transitions, and global policy reform. It situates cannabis as a 
test case for inclusive development, sustainability, and responsible policy transformation 
across diverse socio-ecological realities. 
 Sunny Sweenarain 
A medical cannabis specialist, researcher, and international 
consultant whose work spans the clinical, humanitarian, 
cultural, and ethical dimensions of medical cannabis. He 
studied cannabis science in Canada, grounding his practice
in evidence-based medical knowledge and exposure to 
advanced regulatory contexts. His perspective has been 
shaped by over ten years of work in different developing 
states worldwide.  
Sunny’s work centres on patient-oriented medical cannabis applications, clinical 
interpretation of evidence, and translation of medical knowledge into responsible 
regulatory and practice frameworks. He is committed to supporting individuals with 
chronic conditions — integrating clinical understanding with ethical reflection, 
compassion, and spiritual awareness — while upholding patient dignity, informed 
choice, and continuity of care. He also champions the humanitarian and cultural causes 
of cannabis reform, focusing on addiction prevention and harm reduction. 
Together, the authors bring an intergenerational and interdisciplinary approach to 
Reclaiming the Green Gold: Cannabis at the Crossroads of Health, Justice, and 
Sustainable Development. Their collaboration combines systemic socio-economic 
and governance analysis with grounded clinical, ethical, and community-based insight
