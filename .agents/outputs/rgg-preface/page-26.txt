2.0.2 Policy Pressures and Dynamics 
New dynamics are emerging that further question the adequacy of prohibition as a 
policy framework. Advances in scientific research have complicated earlier 
understandings of cannabis in relation to its medical applications and risk profiles. 
Economic developments, including the rapid growth of legal cannabis markets in some 
jurisdictions, have introduced new incentives and policy considerations. Public attitudes 
have also evolved, with increasing support for approaches that emphasise regulation 
and harm reduction over punitive control (WHO, 2019). 
These developments do not point to a simple replacement of prohibition with 
legalisation. Rather, they signal a move towards more nuanced and differentiated policy 
approaches. In practice, countries are adopting a spectrum of models that combine 
elements of criminal law, public health regulation, and market oversight. This diversity 
reflects the fact that cannabis policy is not a binary choice between prohibition and 
legalisation, but a continuum of governance options. 
2.0.3 A Governance Perspective 
Understanding this transition requires a shift in analytical perspective. Instead of 
focusing solely on legal status, attention must be directed towards how different systems 
of governance operate. This includes examining the design of regulatory frameworks, 
the capacity of institutions to implement them, and the interactions between formal 
policies and informal practices. It also involves recognising that policy outcomes are 
shaped by laws and by broader socio-economic and political dimensions. 
For LMIC, these issues are prominent. In many such contexts, prohibition has been 
unevenly enforced and coexists with informal systems that play an important role in 
livelihoods. Emerging opportunities linked to legal cannabis markets introduce new 
policy dilemmas. Governments must manage the trade-offs between maintaining 
control, enabling economic participation, and managing institutional constraints. 
2.0.4 Structure of the Chapter 
This chapter examines the evolution from prohibition to governance through a 
structured analysis of its key dimensions. Section 2.1 outlines the architecture of the 
prohibition regime and the logic that sustained it. Section 2.2 considers its limitations 
and unintended consequences. Section 2.3 analyses the drivers of policy change. Section 
2.4 introduces the governance continuum as a framework for understanding the 
diversity of policy approaches. Section 2.5 examines emerging regulatory models in 
practice. Section 2.6 addresses policy pathways for LMIC. Section 2.7 synthesises the 
arguments and positions governance as the defining challenge of cannabis policy.
