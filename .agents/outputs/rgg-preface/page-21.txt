1.6 Structure of the Book 
The book is organised into five interrelated parts, each addressing a distinct dimension 
of cannabis as a governance, development, and policy issue. 
Part I — Cannabis at the Crossroads (Chapters 1–5) situates cannabis within its 
historical, social, and policy context. It examines the evolution of control systems, 
cultural dimensions, and the legitimacy and trust challenges underpinning contemporary 
reform debates. This part establishes that effective cannabis policy cannot be designed 
without understanding the deep historical trajectories and cultural meanings that shape 
how societies perceive and govern the plant. 
Part II — Science, Health and Risk Governance (Chapters 6–8) focuses on scientific 
and public health dimensions, providing an overview of biological and therapeutic 
aspects while addressing risk profiles and regulatory implications. It shows that scientific 
evidence is essential but not enough. Effective policy also requires coherent regulation, 
institutional capacity, and equitable access. 
Part III — Sustainable Cannabis Economy (Chapters 9–11) explores the economic and 
industrial dimensions, including value chains, market structures, and trade dynamics, 
with particular attention to sustainability and the integration of green and blue 
economies. This part reveals that economic potential is contingent on governance 
choices and that without deliberate strategies for inclusion and upgrading, legal markets 
risk reproducing familiar patterns of raw-material dependence and external value 
capture.
