2.4.3 Governance Quality over Legal Status 
The continuum also shifts the focus from legal status to the quality of governance. The 
effectiveness of any position along the spectrum depends less on its formal classification 
and more on how it is implemented. Weak governance can undermine even well-
designed regulatory systems, while strong institutions can enhance the performance of 
more limited approaches.

The continuum does not imply a linear progression towards full legalisation. Movement 
along the spectrum is not inherently directional or irreversible. Countries may advance, 
pause, or reverse reforms in response to changing evidence, political dynamics, or 
institutional constraints. This reinforces the importance of adaptive governance, as 
introduced in Chapter 1. 
2.4.4 Applying the Continuum 
The governance continuum can be further operationalised as a conceptual framework 
for policy design and analysis. Three analytical dimensions are useful in interpreting the 
continuum. The first dimension is the degree of state control versus market 
participation. At one end, prohibition and state-controlled systems concentrate 
authority within public institutions. At the commercial end of the spectrum, regulated 
models involve private actors operating under varying levels of oversight.
