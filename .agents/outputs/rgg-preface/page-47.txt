Chapter 3: History of Cannabis  
"Cannabis is one of the five sacred plants that release humans from anxiety. It is a source of 
happiness, a joy-giver, and a liberator."  - Atharvaveda (c. 2000–1400 BCE) 
Abstract 
Cannabis is one of the oldest domesticated plants in human history, embedded across civilisations as a 
medicinal, industrial, and cultural resource. This chapter traces its evolution from early Eurasian 
domestication through Afro-Asian diffusion, colonial transformation, and its eventual incorporation 
into early international control regimes. It shows that cannabis was historically governed through locally 
embedded knowledge systems that regulated its cultivation, preparation, and use within social norms 
rather than formal legal frameworks. 
The chapter argues that the shift to prohibition in the early twentieth century did not emerge from 
scientific consensus but from a convergence of geopolitical interests, colonial governance practices, and 
changing social narratives. This transition disrupted long-standing relationships between societies and 
the plant, replacing adaptive, context-specific systems with rigid, centralised control mechanisms. 
By linking these historical trajectories to contemporary policy challenges, the chapter highlights how 
present-day issues—informality, uneven enforcement, and regulatory fragmentation—are rooted in this 
legacy. Particular attention is given to LMIC and SIDS, where historical patterns of use and production 
continue to shape current realities. This is why understanding cannabis history is not descriptive but 
analytical. It provides essential insights for designing governance systems that are more inclusive, context-
sensitive, and aligned with evolving policy objectives. 
3.0 Introduction 
Cannabis history is not merely a chronological account of uses and practices; it is a 
structural narrative that explains the foundations of contemporary policy systems. 
Historical developments continue to shape how cannabis is perceived, governed, and 
regulated across different societies. The current global framework governing cannabis, 
characterised by prohibition, partial reforms, and emerging regulatory models, cannot 
be fully understood without examining the historical processes that shaped it. 
3.0.1 A Multi-Functional Resource 
Across millennia, cannabis functioned as a multi-purpose plant integrated into diverse 
socio-economic systems. It served simultaneously as a source of fibre for textiles and 
maritime industries, a medicinal resource within early pharmacological traditions, a 
nutritional input through its seeds, and a cultural or ritual medium in various societies 
(Clarke & Merlin, 2016). This multi-functionality positioned cannabis not at the 
margins, but at the centre of everyday life in many regions. Archaeological evidence 
from Central Asia demonstrates that cannabis was among the earliest plants to be 
domesticated, with cultivation dating back more than ten thousand years. In ancient 
China, hemp fibres were essential for textiles, rope, and early paper production, while 
cannabis seeds provided a valuable nutritional resource.
