2.7 Conclusion  
The transition from prohibition to governance in cannabis policy is more than a policy 
adjustment; it reflects a broader shift in how complex social, economic, and regulatory 
issues are understood and managed. As the preceding sections have shown, prohibition 
operates as a structured legal and institutional system, yet its limitations have exposed 
the need for more integrated and adaptive approaches. The focus moves beyond the 
narrow question of legal status towards the broader challenge of building institutional 
capacity, balancing diverse objectives, and sustaining legitimacy over time. 
2.7.1 Suppression of Governance  
The emergence of diverse regulatory models reflects this transition. Rather than 
converging towards a single global standard, countries are adopting a range of 
approaches that combine different levels of legal control, public health protection, 
market regulation, and state oversight. This diversity is not a weakness; it reflects 
differences in institutional capacity, socio-economic conditions, cultural values, and 
national priorities. 
Cannabis governance is inherently context-dependent. Effective policies must respond 
to local realities rather than replicate models developed elsewhere. The objective is 
therefore not to copy a single regulatory framework, but to adapt sound governance 
principles to national circumstances. An adaptive approach, supported by evidence, 
stakeholder engagement, and periodic policy review, enables countries to strengthen 
regulatory performance while responding to evolving public health, economic, and 
social priorities. 
2.7.2 Governance Continuum  
The concept of a governance continuum provides a practical framework for 
understanding this diversity. It recognises that policy choices are not binary but exist 
along a spectrum of regulatory options, each with distinct institutional requirements, 
governance arrangements, and policy implications. Rather than focusing solely on legal 
status, the continuum shifts attention to how regulatory systems function in practice. 
The effectiveness of cannabis policy depends less on whether it is prohibitive or 
permissive than on the quality of governance underpinning its implementation. By 
emphasising system performance over legal labels, the continuum enables policymakers 
to assess trade-offs, sequence reforms, strengthen institutional capacity, and adapt 
regulatory frameworks progressively. This flexible approach encourages evidence-based 
decision-making while avoiding the constraints of rigid ideological positions.
