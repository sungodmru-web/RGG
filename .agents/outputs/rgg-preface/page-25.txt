Chapter 2: From Prohibition to Governance 
"The question is no longer whether to legalise cannabis, but how to govern it effectively in the public 
interest."  Global Commission on Drug Policy (2018) 
Abstract 
Cannabis policy is undergoing a transformative shift from prohibition-based control towards more 
complex systems of governance. This transition reflects growing recognition that prohibition has been only 
partially effective in reducing supply and demand while generating high social, economic, and institutional 
costs. Simultaneously, emerging legal markets and evolving scientific evidence create new policy challenges 
that cannot be addressed through traditional enforcement frameworks alone. 
This chapter examines the evolution from prohibition to governance as a structural transformation in 
public policy. It analyses the historical foundations of prohibition, its limitations, and the factors driving 
reform. It then explores emerging regulatory approaches, highlighting the diversity of models and the 
importance of context-specific design. Special attention is given to LMIC and SIDS, where institutional 
capacity and development priorities shape reform pathways. 
The chapter argues that the primary issue is not whether cannabis should be legal or illegal, but how it 
should be governed. It introduces a governance continuum that moves beyond binary policy choices and 
emphasises the role of institutions, regulation, and adaptive systems. By reframing cannabis policy as a 
governance challenge, the chapter provides a foundation for more effective, balanced, and development-
oriented approaches. 
2.0 Introduction 
Cannabis policy is entering a phase of structural transition. While Chapter 1 established 
the broader context of this shift, the present chapter examines its underlying mechanics: 
how a long-standing prohibition-based system is being reconfigured into more complex 
forms of governance. This transformation is neither linear nor uniform. It reflects a 
combination of historical legacies, institutional constraints, and emerging pressures that 
are reshaping policy choices across jurisdictions. 
2.0.1 Limits of Prohibition Paradigm 
For much of the twentieth century, cannabis control was embedded within a global 
prohibition regime. This regime was anchored in international conventions and 
implemented through national legal systems that prioritised criminalisation and 
enforcement. It provided a coherent but rigid framework that shaped how states 
approached drug control (UNODC, 2023). However, its effectiveness has been 
increasingly questioned. Despite sustained enforcement efforts, cannabis remains one 
of the most widely used controlled substances globally, and illicit markets continue to 
operate at scale. 
The persistence of these outcomes has prompted a reassessment of the underlying 
assumptions of prohibition. It is now widely recognised that enforcement alone cannot 
fully address the complexities of cannabis production, distribution, and use.
