3. Value Capture: The institutional and industrial capacity to retain economic benefits 
domestically through import substitution, export promotion, processing, standards, 
infrastructure and value-chain upgrading. Without deliberate investment in these 
capabilities, producer countries risk falling into the “raw material trap”: supplying 
biomass while higher-value functions remain concentrated elsewhere. 
4. Justice Repair: Mechanisms for addressing historical harms, including expungement, 
community reinvestment, and inclusive pathways for legacy actors. Justice repair 
transforms reform from a legal adjustment into a visible social settlement — one that 
communities can recognise as fair. 
5. Inclusive Finance: Financial systems that enable participation by smallholders, 
cooperatives, women, and SMEs rather than concentrating capital among well-
resourced firms. This layer determines whether the legal market becomes a broad-based 
opportunity or a narrow enclave. 
6. Digital Trust: Traceability, data governance, and cybersecurity systems that build 
regulatory credibility without sacrificing sovereignty. Digital trust is the informational 
backbone of modern cannabis governance. 
7. Adaptive Institutions: The capacity to learn, recalibrate, and maintain public trust 
as conditions evolve. This layer ensures that governance systems remain responsive to 
emerging evidence and shifting dynamics rather than ossifying over time. 
Weakness in any single layer can generate cascading effects that destabilise the entire 
transition. The model functions as both a diagnostic instrument for assessing reform 
readiness and a strategic guide for sequencing governance investments — moving 
policymakers beyond generic templates toward context-specific pathways grounded in 
honest assessment of national capacity. 
Three Practical Annexes 
The book is complemented by three substantive annexes designed to move the analysis 
from argument to application. Annexe 1 — A Snapshot of the Global Cannabis 
Economy brings together key facts, figures, and market signals on demand, production, 
value chains, and growth potential, translating the book’s economic analysis into 
accessible visual summaries. Annexe 2 — A Comparative Analysis of Cannabis 
Governance Models examines how countries have designed and operated their 
regulatory systems — mapping the diversity of approaches from prohibition to medical 
access to commercial legalisation, and drawing out the lessons each model offers for 
jurisdictions at earlier stages of reform. Annexe 3 — The National Cannabis Economy 
Readiness Toolkit provides a guided self-assessment framework across thirteen 
readiness dimensions, enabling governments and stakeholders to diagnose institutional 
strengths, identify critical gaps, and realistically sequence reforms. Together, these 
annexes provide the empirical evidence base and practical instruments for applying, 
testing, and adapting the Governance Transition Model to the national context.
