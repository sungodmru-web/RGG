2.6.5 Policy Risks and Trade-offs 
The design and implementation of cannabis policy involve a series of trade-offs that 
must be carefully managed. These trade-offs are significant in LMIC, where institutional 
and economic constraints are more pronounced. One key trade-off lies between 
economic development and public health objectives. Expanding commercial markets 
may generate employment and revenue, but over-commercialisation can increase 
consumption and associated risks. Balancing these objectives entails well-designed 
regulatory measures, including control on advertising, potency limits, and pricing.  
A second risk is market concentration and exclusion. Without deliberate safeguards, 
cannabis markets may become dominated by well-capitalised actors, including foreign 
investors. This can marginalise small-scale producers and limit the distribution of 
economic benefits. Inclusive licensing, local ownership requirements, and support for 
cooperative models can help mitigate this risk. 
Regulatory capture represents another potential challenge. Complex regulatory systems 
can be influenced by vested interests where oversight mechanisms are weak. Hence, 
ensuring transparency and accountability is essential. Fiscal dependence is also a 
concern. Governments may become reliant on cannabis-related revenues, creating 
incentives to prioritise fiscal objectives over health or social considerations. Diversified 
economic strategies are needed to mitigate this risk.
