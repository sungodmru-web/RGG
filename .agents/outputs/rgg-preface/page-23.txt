Chapter 1 - Cannabis in the 21st Century 
11 
1. Reframe cannabis as a governance and development issue. Cannabis policy 
should be integrated into broader frameworks of economic development, public health, 
and environmental sustainability. Anchoring cannabis within national development 
strategies, across agriculture, industrial policy, and green and blue economy agendas, 
strengthens both coherence and long-term impact. It helps align sectoral policies and 
mobilise institutions towards shared national objectives. 
2. Align reform pathways with institutional capacity. Ambitious regulatory models 
are unlikely to succeed where administrative and enforcement capacity is limited. 
Governments should adopt phased approaches that match policy complexity to 
institutional capacity, expanding regulatory systems as capacity grows. Progressive 
implementation builds credibility while reducing implementation risks. 
3. Promote inclusive participation in emerging value chains. Cannabis markets risk 
concentration among well-capitalised actors without deliberate measures to support 
domestic participation. Policies should facilitate access for smallholders, cooperatives, 
and local enterprises through appropriate licensing frameworks, technical support, and 
access to finance. Broader participation strengthens local ownership and spreads 
economic benefits more widely and equitably. 
4. Move beyond primary production towards value addition. Capturing greater 
value requires moving into processing, manufacturing, and innovation. Governments 
should encourage local value addition through incentives, infrastructure development, 
and skills upgrading to avoid remaining confined to low-value segments of the supply 
chain. Developing competitive local industries can also improve economic integration, 
resilience, and export potential. 
5. Strengthen regulatory systems and data capacity. Effective governance depends 
on the ability to monitor, regulate, and adapt. Investment in licensing, quality assurance, 
traceability, and data systems is essential for informed decision-making and policy 
responsiveness. Reliable data strengthens transparency, accountability, and public trust. 
6. Integrate environmental sustainability into policy design. Cannabis cultivation 
and processing must align with ecological objectives, predominantly in resource- 
constrained and climate-sensitive contexts. Policies should incentivise environmentally 
responsible practices. Sustainable production protects natural resources while 
supporting long-term sector competitiveness and resilience. 
7. Manage risks of external dependency and regulatory capture. Safeguards are 
needed to ensure national interests are protected as global markets expand, including 
transparent licensing, limits on market concentration, and clear investment frameworks. 
Strong oversight promotes fair competition and preserves public trust in the reform 
process. 
8. Adopt adaptive and iterative policy approaches. Given evolving markets and 
scientific knowledge, policies should incorporate mechanisms for evaluation, feedback, 
and revision. Pilot programmes, phased implementation, and continuous learning can 
reduce risks and improve outcomes. Regular policy reviews help ensure that regulation 
remains effective and responsive to changing conditions.
