Global & Regional Partnerships

Framed around institutional cooperation — connecting knowledge, policy, and practice across continents and regional communities.

Institutional Partners

Seven Pillars of Regional Cooperation

African Union Commission

55 Member States • Continental

The AU's 2026–2030 drug policy framework presents a historic opportunity to treat cannabis as a governance, development, and trade issue aligned with Agenda 2063 and the AfCFTA.

  • Pharmaceutical localisation and agro-industrialisation
  • Continental standards harmonisation
  • Shared laboratory infrastructure

The 8 AU-Recognised RECs

Regional Economic Communities • Africa

The eight RECs — including SADC, ECOWAS, EAC, COMESA, and IGAD — serve as operational platforms. Shared laboratories and harmonised standards can reduce per-country compliance costs by an estimated 40–60%.

  • Standards and certification coordination
  • Pooled laboratory and testing capacity
  • Trade facilitation and customs harmonisation

CARICOM

15 Member States • Caribbean Community

CARICOM has been one of the most normatively distinctive regional voices. The Regional Commission on Marijuana (2018) linked reform to social justice, public health, and cultural legitimacy. For SIDS, CARICOM's coordination reduces isolation through shared facilities and mutual recognition.

  • Social justice and reparative framing
  • Shared standards and laboratory capacity for SIDS
  • Cultural and traditional knowledge recognition

ASEAN

10 Member States • Southeast Asia

ASEAN presents a complex mosaic — from Thailand's liberalisation to Singapore's prohibition. The absence of a coordinated ASEAN framework creates both space for national experimentation and risks of regulatory fragmentation.

  • Comparative policy learning across diverse models
  • Regional investment and trade coherence
  • Public health and security coordination

MERCOSUR

5 Member States • South America

MERCOSUR states navigate varied frameworks — from Uruguay's state-controlled model to Brazil's medical pathways. Regional cooperation strengthens regulatory coherence and reduces trade friction.

  • Regulatory coherence and mutual recognition
  • Cost-competitive production and export coordination
  • Constitutional and human rights alignment

The Commonwealth Secretariat

56 Member States • Global

The Commonwealth provides a unique platform for dialogue among developed and developing nations, including many SIDS and African countries. The Secretariat's work on small states and sustainable development aligns with the governance challenges addressed in Reclaiming the Green Gold.

  • Small-state and SIDS-focused policy support
  • Trade and standards capacity-building
  • South–South and North–South dialogue

Organisation Internationale de la Francophonie (OIF)

88 Member States and Governments • Global

The OIF spans Africa, the Caribbean, Europe, and the Indo-Pacific. Its emphasis on cultural diversity, legal cooperation, and sustainable development supports the French edition, Reconquérir l'Or Vert.

  • French-language knowledge dissemination
  • Legal and institutional cooperation
  • Cultural and traditional knowledge recognition
Cooperation Framework

Why Regional Cooperation Matters

Strategic Rationale

For LMIC and SIDS, regional cooperation is often a developmental necessity. Small markets struggle to build full laboratory ecosystems and bargaining power alone. CARICOM's Regional Commission on Marijuana exemplifies how SIDS can leverage collective action. Regional integration pools capacity, harmonises standards, and lowers compliance costs.

Practical Mechanisms

  • Shared laboratories and certification: Regional reference laboratories reduce per-country costs. CARICOM's CROSQ is developing regional cannabis standards.
  • Harmonised standards: Common GMP, GACP, and quality standards reduce trade friction.
  • Pooled training: Regional platforms for regulator training — particularly valuable for SIDS.
  • Collective trade negotiation: Coordinated positioning strengthens bargaining power.
