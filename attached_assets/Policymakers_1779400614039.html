Policy Guidance

Eight Guiding Considerations for LMIC and SIDS

1

Reframe Cannabis as a Governance and Development Issue

Cannabis policy should be integrated into broader frameworks of economic development, public health, and environmental sustainability. Position cannabis within national development strategies — particularly agriculture, industrial policy, and green and blue economy agendas.

2

Align Reform Pathways with Institutional Capacity

Ambitious regulatory models are unlikely to succeed where administrative and enforcement capacity is limited. Adopt phased approaches matching policy complexity with institutional readiness.

3

Promote Inclusive Participation in Emerging Value Chains

Cannabis markets risk concentration among well-capitalised actors without deliberate measures to support domestic participation. Facilitate access for smallholders, cooperatives, and local enterprises.

4

Move Beyond Primary Production Towards Value Addition

Capturing greater value requires moving into processing, manufacturing, and innovation. Encourage local value addition through incentives, infrastructure development, and skills upgrading.

5

Strengthen Regulatory Systems and Data Capacity

Effective governance depends on the ability to monitor, regulate, and adapt. Investment in licensing, quality assurance, traceability, and data systems is essential.

6

Integrate Environmental Sustainability into Policy Design

Cannabis cultivation and processing must align with environmental objectives, particularly in resource-constrained and climate-vulnerable contexts.

7

Manage Risks of External Dependency and Regulatory Capture

Safeguards are needed to ensure national interests are protected — including transparent licensing, limits on market concentration, and clear investment frameworks.

8

Adopt Adaptive and Iterative Policy Approaches

Given evolving markets and scientific knowledge, policies should incorporate mechanisms for evaluation, feedback, and revision. Pilot programmes and continuous learning reduce risks.

Knowledge & Capacity-Building

Building Institutional Capability

Cannabis reform is a test of institutional capability, policy coherence, and social contract. The book's central finding is that developmental outcomes are determined by governance design, not market size, climate, or comparative advantage.

Regulatory & Institutional Strengthening

Clear mandates, inter-agency coordination, licensing systems, inspection capacity, and anti-capture safeguards.

Scientific & Laboratory Infrastructure

Accredited testing facilities, GMP certification pathways, pharmacovigilance systems, and research partnerships.

Data Systems & Digital Governance

Seed-to-sale traceability, licensing portals, market monitoring databases, and evidence-based policy frameworks.

Market & Value Chain Development

Domestic processing capacity, SME support, cooperative models, export readiness, and fair taxation frameworks.

Public Health & Social Protection

Patient access pathways, prevention programmes, harm reduction strategies, and human rights safeguards.

Regional Cooperation Platforms

Shared laboratories, harmonised standards, pooled training, mutual recognition of certification, and collective trade negotiation.

Technical Assistance

Support for Policy Development & Implementation

How the Book Serves as a Technical Resource

Reclaiming the Green Gold is designed as a practical instrument for governments, regional organisations, and development partners. It provides:

  • The Cannabis Governance Transition Model — a seven-layer diagnostic framework.
  • The National Cannabis Economy Self-Assessment Framework — a guided toolkit across 13 readiness dimensions.
  • Comparative case studies from Lesotho, Morocco, Jamaica, Colombia, Thailand, Uruguay, and Canada.
  • Annexes with practical instruments — Global Economy Snapshot, Comparative Governance Models, and Priority Action Matrix.

Core Principles for Technical Assistance

  • Sequence reform around institutional readiness, not market ambition.
  • Use regional cooperation as a strategic platform.
  • Treat equity and inclusion as core design principles.
  • Invest in adaptive governance systems.
  • Apply the Governance Transition Model as a diagnostic tool.

"The window for deliberate design is open. Countries which invest in the seven governance layers now — before markets consolidate, before regulatory capture sets in, before the political moment passes — will not merely regulate cannabis better. They will have built governance infrastructure capable of navigating whatever complex transition comes next." — Reclaiming the Green Gold
