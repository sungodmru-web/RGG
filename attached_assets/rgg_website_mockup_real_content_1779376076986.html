The Book · 5 Parts · 20 Chapters
A Governance Handbook, Not a Legal Manifesto
Cannabis reform is not a binary legal choice between prohibition and legalisation — it is a governance transition. Outcomes depend less on legal status than on the quality of institutions, coherence of policies, and adaptive learning capacity states bring to implementation.
I
Cannabis at the Crossroads Ch. 1–5
History, Culture, and Legitimacy as Hidden Foundations. Legitimacy is foundational — where formal rules align with cultural norms, compliance follows. Reform design must begin with historical and cultural literacy.
II
Science, Health & Risk Governance Ch. 6–8
From Prohibition to Evidence-Based Policy. The endocannabinoid system — a fundamental physiological regulator — repositions cannabis within human biology. Therapeutic potential for chronic pain, epilepsy, and chemotherapy-induced nausea examined with clear-eyed rigour.
III
Sustainable Cannabis Economy Ch. 9–11
Cultivation accounts for only 10–20% of final product value. The remaining 80–90% is in processing, extraction, formulation, branding. Producer countries risk the "raw material trap" — supplying biomass while value is captured elsewhere. This outcome is not inevitable. It is a governance choice.
IV
Governance & Development Pathways Ch. 12–17
Regulatory design, justice repair, institutions, and finance. Eight country archetypes: Canada, Uruguay, Germany, Jamaica, Lesotho, Morocco, Colombia, South Africa. Communities that bore prohibition's heaviest costs are owed recognition, repair, and genuine participation.
V
System Design & Futures Ch. 18–20
Implementation, digital sovereignty, adaptive learning. For developing countries: the imperative to avoid foreign-hosted traceability platforms that harvest commercial intelligence and become chokepoints. Regional cooperation as collective digital statecraft.
The Framework · The Cannabis Governance Transition Model
Seven Interdependent Governance Layers
Weakness in any single layer can generate cascading effects that destabilise the entire transition. The model functions as both an assessment tool and a guide for sequencing governance investments.
01
Evidence Credibility
Capacity to generate, interpret, and apply scientific data to inform policy and prevent ideology.
02
Public Health
Balancing therapeutic access with proportionate safeguards and harm reduction across populations.
03
Value Capture
Retaining economic benefits domestically through processing, standards, infrastructure, value-chain upgrading.
04
Justice Repair
Expungement, community reinvestment, inclusive pathways for legacy actors. Transforms reform into a visible social settlement.
05
Inclusive Finance
Enabling smallholders, cooperatives, women, and SMEs — not concentrating capital among well-resourced firms.
06
Digital Trust
Traceability, data governance, and cybersecurity that build regulatory credibility without sacrificing sovereignty.
07
Adaptive Legitimacy
Capacity to learn, recalibrate, and maintain public trust as conditions evolve. The backbone of durable reform.
