Core Framework

The Cannabis Governance Transition Model

The book's cumulative argument is distilled into a seven-layer diagnostic framework. These layers interact to create a multiplier effect — and a cascade risk. Weakness in any single layer can generate cascading effects that destabilise the entire transition. The model functions as both a diagnostic instrument for assessing reform readiness and a strategic guide for sequencing governance investments.

1

Evidence Credibility

The capacity to generate, interpret, and apply scientific and administrative data to inform policy and public communication. Without credible evidence systems, policy becomes vulnerable to ideology and misinformation.

2

Differentiated Public Health

The ability to balance therapeutic access with proportionate safeguards, prevention, and harm reduction calibrated to the actual distribution of risk across populations and products.

3

Value Capture

The institutional and industrial capacity to retain economic benefits domestically through processing, standards infrastructure, and upgrading. Without deliberate investment, producer countries risk the "raw material trap."

4

Justice Repair

Mechanisms for addressing historical harms, including expungement, community reinvestment, and inclusive pathways for legacy actors. Justice repair transforms reform from a legal adjustment into a visible social settlement.

5

Inclusive Finance

Financial systems that enable participation by smallholders, cooperatives, women, and SMEs rather than concentrating capital among well-resourced firms. This layer determines whether the legal market becomes a broad-based opportunity or a narrow enclave.

6

Digital Trust

Traceability, data governance, and cybersecurity systems that build regulatory credibility without sacrificing sovereignty. Digital trust is the informational backbone of modern cannabis governance.

7

Adaptive Institutional Legitimacy

The capacity to learn, recalibrate, and maintain public trust as conditions evolve. This layer ensures governance systems remain responsive to emerging evidence and shifting dynamics rather than ossifying over time.

Practical Diagnostic Tool

National Cannabis Economy Self-Assessment Framework

A guided toolkit for multi-ministry teams, organised across 13 readiness dimensions in three clusters. The framework helps governments assess institutional strengths, identify critical gaps, and sequence reforms realistically — following the logic: Assess → Score → Interpret → Prioritise → Act → Review.

Cluster A — Governance Foundations

1. National Vision, Strategy & Policy CoherenceIs there a clear national direction, shared across ministries and linked to development priorities?
2. Legal & Regulatory FrameworkAre cannabis and hemp categories clearly defined and supported by operational regulations?
3. Institutional & Governance CapacityIs there a capable lead authority with mandate, budget, technical capacity, and accountability safeguards?

Cluster B — People, Economy & Environment

4. Public Health, Human Rights & Social ProtectionDoes the system protect patients, reduce harm, and avoid disproportionate criminalisation?
5. Economic, Industrial & Market DevelopmentDoes the market structure support SMEs, value addition, fair taxation, and export readiness?
6. Agriculture, Environment & SustainabilityAre farmers supported, local genetic resources protected, and environmental impacts regulated?
7. Social Equity, Inclusion & Community ParticipationAre affected communities, women, youth, smallholders, and traditional growers meaningfully included?

Cluster C — Enablers, Accountability & Alignment

8. Knowledge, Data, Innovation & Digital SystemsAre data, research, and digital tools used to support evidence-based regulation?
9. Cultural, Spiritual & Recreational DimensionsAre cultural realities, traditional use, and recreational patterns addressed honestly and proportionately?
10. Monitoring, Evaluation, Learning & AccountabilityAre outcomes tracked, independently reviewed, and used to improve the system?
11. International & Regional AlignmentIs the country compliant with international obligations and engaged in regional cooperation?
12. Community Participation, FPIC & Local GovernanceAre local communities consulted, protected, and empowered in decisions affecting them?
13. SDG & Green Economy AlignmentDoes the sector contribute measurably to sustainable development, climate action, and institutional integrity?
