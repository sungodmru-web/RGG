Synopsis

Reclaiming the Green Gold is the first comprehensive, evidence-based guide to cannabis governance written for policymakers, institutions, and development practitioners — particularly those operating in low- and middle-income countries and Small Island Developing States. It reframes cannabis not as a vice or a commodity, but as a cross-cutting governance challenge at the intersection of public health, criminal justice reform, economic development, and international cooperation.

Drawing on comparative analysis from Africa, Asia, Latin America, and Europe, the book provides a neutral, institutional framework for countries navigating the transition from prohibition to regulated governance. At its heart lies the Cannabis Governance Transition Model — a seven-layer diagnostic framework for assessing institutional readiness, sequencing reform, and building systems capable of learning and self-improvement.

Across twenty chapters and three practical annexes, the book examines the full arc of the cannabis question — from its deep roots in ancient medicine and culture, through the scientific, economic, and public health dimensions, to the governance architectures that will determine whether reform delivers broad-based development or narrow extraction.

Overview

The Book at a Glance

In 2021, a smallholder farmer in Lesotho harvested what should have been a profitable cannabis crop. The plant was legal. The licence was valid. But without a functioning export certification system, a credible testing laboratory, or access to formal finance, the harvest went nowhere. The problem was never the cannabis — it was the governance. This book is about that gap: the space between legal reform and durable outcomes, between policy ambition and institutional reality.

Core Analytical Framework

Three interlocking principles structure the analysis. Differentiation recognises that countries operate under distinct historical, economic, and institutional conditions. Institutional readiness holds that reform ambition must align with administrative capacity. Adaptive governance treats policy as an evolving process of calibration, learning, and recalibration under conditions of persistent uncertainty.

The Cannabis Governance Transition Model

The book's cumulative argument is distilled into a seven-layer diagnostic framework — covering Evidence Credibility, Differentiated Public Health, Value Capture, Justice Repair, Inclusive Finance, Digital Trust, and Adaptive Institutional Legitimacy. The model functions as both a diagnostic instrument and a strategic guide for sequencing governance investments.

Three Practical Annexes

The book is complemented by three substantive annexes: A Snapshot of the Global Cannabis Economy, A Comparative Analysis of Cannabis Governance Models, and The National Cannabis Economy Readiness Toolkit — a guided self-assessment framework across thirteen readiness dimensions.

Inside the Book

5 Interconnected Parts — 20 Chapters

I

Cannabis at the Crossroads

Part I situates cannabis historically, culturally, and politically — framing reform as a governance transition rather than a binary legal choice, tracing the evolution from prohibition's rigid architecture to a governance continuum, and revealing cannabis as a medicinal, industrial, and ritual resource across civilisations.

  • Frames cannabis reform as a transition from prohibition-based control to governance-oriented systems. Argues that legal change alone is insufficient and that outcomes depend on institutional capacity, policy design, and implementation. Introduces three guiding principles — differentiation, institutional readiness, and adaptive governance.
  • Examines the evolution from prohibition to governance as a structural transformation in public policy. Analyses the historical foundations of prohibition, its limitations, and the factors driving reform. Introduces a governance continuum that moves beyond binary policy choices.
  • Traces cannabis from early domestication through Afro-Eurasian diffusion, colonial transformation, and incorporation into international control regimes. Shows that cannabis was historically governed through locally embedded knowledge systems disrupted by twentieth-century prohibition.
  • Offers a global ethnography documenting cannabis as healer, sacrament, and cultural companion across Africa, Asia, the Middle East, the Americas, and Europe. Examines cultural resilience under prohibition and cannabis through the UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage framework.
  • Examines cannabis as a site of epistemic contestation where scientific, traditional, and experiential knowledge interact and conflict. Explores how colonialism and prohibition established hierarchies that privileged certain forms of knowledge while marginalising others.
II

Science, Health and Risk Governance

Part II examines cannabis through the integrated lenses of scientific discovery, clinical application, and public health — from the isolation of THC and CBD to the discovery of the Endocannabinoid System, and from therapeutic evidence to population-level risk governance.

  • Traces the scientific rediscovery of cannabis — from the isolation of THC and CBD to the discovery of the Endocannabinoid System. Examines the "lost century" of interrupted research, the tension between pharmaceutical and whole-plant models, and knowledge justice.
  • Examines medical and therapeutic applications through an integrated lens connecting scientific evidence, clinical practice, and health system governance. Reviews evidence for chronic pain, neurological disorders, epilepsy, and palliative care.
  • Examines cannabis through the dual lens of public health and risk governance. Analyses epidemiological trends, health risks, prevention strategies, and harm reduction approaches. Introduces risk governance as a central concept for managing uncertainty.
III

Sustainable Cannabis Economy

Part III examines the cannabis economy through economic governance, production systems, and global market dynamics — establishing that economic outcomes are shaped by deliberate policy choices, institutional capacity, and strategic positioning within asymmetrical global markets.

  • Examines the emergence of the cannabis economy as a domain of economic transformation shaped by governance, public health, and sustainability. Explores formalisation pathways, value chain governance, and structural asymmetries in global markets.
  • Examines industrial hemp as a strategic component of the cannabis economy and a driver of green industrialisation. Analyses applications across textiles, construction, bio-materials, and its environmental performance and contribution to rural industrialisation.
  • Examines how value is created, governed, and captured across the global cannabis economy. Analyses trade dynamics, standards, compliance burdens, and risks of market concentration. Positions cannabis as a test case in development governance.
IV

Governance and Development Pathways

Part IV examines the governance architecture through which cannabis reform is operationalised — demonstrating that durable reform depends on coherent systems aligning regulatory design, institutional capacity, inclusive participation, and sustainable finance.

  • Examines the main typologies of cannabis regulation and the policy choices that shape them. Analyses licensing systems, market controls, public and private models, and trade-offs among public health, commercialisation, inclusion, and administrative feasibility.
  • Examines the evolution of the international drug control regime and the legal pathways through which cannabis governance is being reinterpreted. Explores treaty flexibilities, constitutional approaches, and managed non-compliance.
  • Examines how institutional architecture shapes governance outcomes. Compares centralised authorities, fragmented systems, and hybrid models. Argues that institutional governance is the critical bridge between legal reform and durable outcomes.
  • Examines cannabis as a development question — exploring structural transformation, Africa's evolving position, SIDS opportunities, the dangers of extractive models, and the role of regional integration.
  • Examines cannabis reform through the lens of justice, inclusion, and redress. Argues that justice is a condition of legitimacy, and that post-prohibition systems fail when they replace criminal exclusion with legal-market exclusion.
  • Examines the financial architecture of the cannabis economy — investment models, fiscal regimes, risk management, and sustainable financing. Argues that without credible financial architecture, legalisation remains symbolic.
V

System Design and Futures

Part V turns to implementation and forward-looking analysis — examining policy sequencing, evidence systems, adaptive governance, and future scenarios. It argues that implementation is where reform is truly tested.

  • Examines sequencing, transition pathways, pilots, adaptive rollout, risk management, and low-capacity implementation. Argues that implementation is a test of state capability and governance intelligence.
  • Examines cannabis governance through evidence, indicators, monitoring, evaluation, learning, and adaptive adjustment. Argues that successful reform requires systems capable of tracking outcomes and responding intelligently.
  • Synthesises the book's core argument. Presents the Cannabis Governance Transition Model as a transferable framework, explores future scenarios, and positions cannabis as a lens for understanding broader challenges of governing complex transitions.

About the Authors

SS

Dr Soobaschand (Sunil) Sweenarain

Natural Resource Economist

Dr Sweenarain is a senior natural resource economist with over three decades of international experience in sustainable development, renewable resource management, economic governance, and policy advisory. Born and raised in Mauritius, he has centred his career primarily on the African continent, where he has led and advised on complex programmes in resource management, regional cooperation, and economic integration. Grounded in sustainable development and the blue-green economy paradigm, he has provided expertise in large-scale project design, institutional and human capacity building, and strategic policy advisory services.

SS

Sunny Sweenarain

Medical Cannabis Specialist

Sunny Sweenarain is a medical cannabis specialist, researcher, and international consultant whose work spans the clinical, humanitarian, cultural, and ethical dimensions of medical cannabis. He studied cannabis science in Canada, grounding his practice in evidence-based medical knowledge and exposure to advanced regulatory environments. With over a decade of experience, his perspective is further shaped by work in various developing states worldwide. Sunny's work centres on patient-oriented medical cannabis applications, clinical interpretation of evidence, and translation of medical knowledge into responsible regulatory and practice frameworks.
