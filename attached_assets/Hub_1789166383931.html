Policy BriefMarch 2026

North American Cannabis Markets: Consolidation, Equity, and Illicit Competition

North America remains the dominant regulated cannabis market, accounting for approximately 70% of global legal sales (Annexe 2). Canada's federal legalisation in 2018 established the first G7 regulated adult-use system, while the United States presents a fragmented landscape of state-level legalisation under federal prohibition.

🇨🇦 Canada🇺🇸 United States

Canada: Consolidation and Illicit Persistence

Canada's legal market has generated over CAD 10 billion in investment. However, the top five licensed producers control approximately 60% of domestic market share (Ch. 11). Despite federal legalisation, an estimated 30–40% of cannabis purchases continued through illicit channels in 2023, driven by price differentials (Ch. 17).

United States: Federal–State Fragmentation

The US exemplifies a fragmented subnational model with cannabis remaining Schedule I under federal law while individual states implement medical and adult-use frameworks. Several states have pioneered social equity programmes, though implementation challenges persist (Ch. 12; Ch. 16).

Sources: Reclaiming the Green Gold — Chapters 2, 11, 12, 16, 17; Annexe 2; Annexe 3.

Policy BriefMarch 2026

European Cannabis Governance: Medical Leadership, Incremental Reform, and Regulatory Pluralism

Europe represents the largest medical cannabis import market globally, with Germany alone accounting for over 40% of EU medical cannabis imports (Ch. 11). The region is characterised by regulated pluralism.

🇩🇪 Germany🇵🇹 Portugal🇳🇱 Netherlands🇲🇹 Malta🇨🇭 Switzerland

Germany: Pharmaceutical Leadership

Germany's medical cannabis programme allows reimbursement through statutory health insurance. The country's evolving two-pillar model reflects an adaptive, evidence-building approach (Ch. 12; Ch. 13).

Portugal, the Netherlands, and Malta

Portugal's 2001 decriminalisation remains a landmark policy experiment, and the country has emerged as a significant medical cannabis exporter (Ch. 11). Malta's 2021 reform established a framework for non-profit cannabis associations (Ch. 13).

Sources: Reclaiming the Green Gold — Chapters 2, 7, 11, 12, 13; Tables 13, 24; Annexe 3.

Policy BriefFebruary 2026

Latin America: Cost Advantage, Regulatory Innovation, and the Challenge of Inclusion

Latin America has emerged as one of the most dynamic regions in the global cannabis economy. Colombia and Uruguay represent distinct but instructive reform pathways.

🇨🇴 Colombia🇺🇾 Uruguay🇲🇽 Mexico🇧🇷 Brazil

Colombia: Cost Leader with Inclusion Challenges

Colombia has attracted over USD 2 billion in investment, with ex-farm production costs at USD 0.05–0.10 per gram — the lowest among major exporters (Ch. 11). However, smallholder inclusion remains elusive (Ch. 15).

Uruguay: State-Controlled Public Health Model

Uruguay's 2013 legalisation established the first nationally regulated adult-use system prioritising public health over commercial development. Implementation was deliberately phased (Ch. 12; Ch. 18).

Sources: Reclaiming the Green Gold — Chapters 2, 11, 12, 13, 15, 18; Tables 24, 26, 47; Annexe 2; Annexe 3.

Africa's Cannabis Crossroads: Why the Continent Needs a Governance Framework

Over the past decade, at least ten African countries have enacted legislation to regulate cannabis production. These reforms are unfolding largely in isolation — no harmonised standards, no shared testing laboratories, no mutual recognition protocols, and growing concerns about corporate capture excluding smallholder farmers.

🇱🇸 Lesotho🇿🇦 South Africa🇲🇦 Morocco🇿🇼 Zimbabwe🇲🇼 Malawi🇷🇼 Rwanda🇬🇭 Ghana🇿🇲 Zambia🇺🇬 Uganda🇸🇿 Eswatini

A Continent in Motion

Lesotho was first to legalise medical cannabis export in 2017. Morocco established ANRAC as a dedicated regulatory authority. South Africa is developing a National Cannabis Master Plan targeting 130,000 jobs. Malawi has licensed 35 companies as it diversifies from tobacco (Ch. 15).

The Coordination Gap

There are currently no harmonised testing standards, no shared reference laboratories, and no mutual recognition protocols across African nations. Establishing a single accredited laboratory requires USD 500,000–2 million (Ch. 14; Ch. 17).

The AU's 2026–2030 Drug Policy Framework

The African Union's new framework presents a historic opportunity to treat cannabis as a governance, development, and trade issue aligned with Agenda 2063 and the AfCFTA.

Sources: Reclaiming the Green Gold — Chapters 2, 9, 11, 13, 14, 15, 16, 17, 20; Tables 17, 22, 24, 47; Annexe 2; Annexe 3; Annexe 4.

Data InsightJanuary 2026

Global Cannabis Economy: Key Statistics and Growth Projections

This data insight synthesises key statistics from the book's annexes and chapters.

Market Size and Structure

The global legal cannabis market was valued at approximately USD 35–40 billion in 2024, with projections exceeding USD 100 billion by 2030 (Ch. 11; Annexe 2). Medical cannabis accounts for ~60% of legal cross-border trade, industrial hemp ~25%, and CBD/wellness ~15% (Ch. 11).

Production Economics

Ex-farm costs range from USD 0.05–0.10/gram (Colombia) to USD 0.80–1.50/gram (Canada). GMP certification for a single facility requires USD 1–5 million (Ch. 11).

Growth Scenarios

The book presents three scenarios: conservative (USD 60–70B), base case (USD 100–120B), and accelerated (USD 150B+) by 2030 (Annexe 2).

Sources: Reclaiming the Green Gold — Chapters 9, 10, 11; Tables 22, 23, 24; Annexe 2; Annexe 3.
